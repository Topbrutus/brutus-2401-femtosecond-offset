# Residual Propagation — Version 2.0.0

## Purpose

Version 1 documented the exact candidate frequency generated by a 1 femtosecond period-offset hypothesis. Version 2 follows the small residual explicitly instead of stopping at the rounded reference value.

Define

\[
f_0=240.1,
\qquad
\delta T=10^{-15},
\qquad
f=\frac{f_0}{1-f_0\delta T}.
\]

Then

\[
\Delta f=f-f_0
\approx5.7648010000013841287201003323\times10^{-11}.
\]

For the dimensionless scaling factor

\[
N=2401,
\]

the reference and candidate propagate as

\[
Nf_0=576480.1
\]

and

\[
Nf
\approx576480.1000001384128720100332329305696.
\]

The difference is therefore

\[
\Delta_N=Nf-Nf_0
=N\Delta f
\approx1.3841287201003323293056960898\times10^{-7}.
\]

## What changed in v2

The numerical value \(576480.1000001384\ldots\) is no longer treated as though the trailing digits were an unrelated numerical artifact. Under the stated construction, their origin is traceable to the original \(240.1\) residual and the multiplication by \(2401\).

This is a bookkeeping and derivation improvement. It does **not** create a new physical constant and does **not** establish a physical correspondence to the Brout-Englert-Higgs boson or to any other measured particle property.

## Reproducibility rule

A downstream calculation that uses the exact candidate should propagate the exact value at the working precision chosen for the calculation. A downstream calculation that intentionally uses the rounded reference \(240.1\) should label that approximation explicitly.

The two should not be mixed without recording the resulting residual.
